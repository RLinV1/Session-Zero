# Cairnkeepers — Rules

*A game of rope, weather and the safe way through, for a GM and 2–5 players.*

## 1. The Whitecrown

For three hundred years the guild of **Cairnkeepers** has walked travellers over the Whitecrown
pass. Above the treeline there is no road, only snow, and the way is marked by **cairns**: stone
piles a little taller than a man, one in sight of the next. A Cairnkeeper keeps them standing,
reads the weather, carries the rope, and brings people down alive.

This winter came early. Cairns that stood for a century have been found knocked flat, or moved,
leading onto thin ice and over edges. The guild says it is the Rimehands, the wreckers who loot
what the mountain takes. The old guides say something else moves the stones, and that it has
always lived up there, under the ice, waiting for the cairns to fall.

You play **Guides** of the guild. You go up when nobody else will.

**What the game is about.** Two things decide every scene: **the ground** (where you can stand,
how fast you move, what gives way under you) and **what you can see** (the weather, the rock in
the way, the white that hides the wolves). Both are on the board, and the board enforces them.

## 2. Dice

Everything is rolled on **2d6**, plus one of your four **traits**.

| Trait | What it covers |
|---|---|
| **Grit** | Strength, endurance, cold, carrying, hitting hard |
| **Footing** | Balance, climbing, ice, quick hands, getting out of the way |
| **Sense** | Spotting, tracking, reading the weather, aiming |
| **Lore** | Rope-work, medicine, the old ways, the Stonespeakers' arts |

A trait runs from −1 to +3.

### 2.1 Checks

When the outcome is in doubt and failing would matter, roll **2d6 + trait** against a target:

| Target | |
|---|---|
| **8** | Standard. A trained Guide makes this more often than not. |
| **10** | Hard. |
| **12** | Extreme. Only the best, with help. |

Meet or beat it and you succeed. A roll of **2** (double ones) always fails; a roll of **12**
(double sixes) always succeeds.

**Knacks.** Each Guide has two Knacks, things they are trained in: *Climbing, Ropework, Tracking,
Weather-wise, Medicine, Stealth, Cairn-lore, Hauling, Ice-craft, Hunting, Cooking, Haggling.* A
Knack that applies adds **+1**.

**Edge and Snag.** Something in your favour (high ground, a Mark, a good plan, help from an ally)
is an **Edge**, +1. Something against you (cover, the wind, a bad footing) is a **Snag**, −1. They
stack up to ±2 and cancel each other. The GM calls them.

> **On the board.** Players roll their own checks in the Dice panel (`2d6+2`). For attacks, the
> GM folds Edge and Snag into the hit-or-miss ruling, so nobody re-rolls.

### 2.2 Saves

When something happens *to* you (an avalanche, a breath of killing cold) you **save**: 2d6 + the
trait named, against the effect's difficulty (**DC**), usually 8 or 10. Many effects do half
damage on a save.

> **On the board.** A save against an area is an **area effect**: the GM places the template,
> starts an Area Effect with the expression `2d6`, and the server rolls it for every token caught.
> The GM adds each one's trait, then marks each target **Full** or **Half**.

## 3. Making a Guide

1. **Choose a calling** (§9): Pathfinder, Hauler, Hunter or Stonespeaker.
2. **Assign traits**: +2, +1, 0, 0 in any order, then add your calling's +1. No trait may start
   above +3.
3. **Choose a background** and take its Knack, then choose one more Knack:

   | Background | Knack |
   |---|---|
   | Guild-born | Cairn-lore |
   | Salt-trader | Haggling |
   | Hill farmer | Hauling |
   | Reformed Rimehand | Stealth |
   | Hospice orphan | Medicine |
   | Surveyor's apprentice | Weather-wise |

4. **Work out your numbers**: HP from your calling; **Guard** = 7 + Footing + armour (heavy coat
   +1; a shield +1); **Move** 6 squares.
5. **Take your gear** (§9 and §10), your rope, and two bonds: a short sentence about another Guide.

## 4. The ground

The board is divided into 5 ft squares. Every square is one of three kinds of ground, and the
board itself knows which.

| Ground | On the board | What it is up here | Moving |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Normal** | nothing painted | packed trail, rock floor, boards, safe ice | 1 square per square |
| **Difficult** | painted Difficult | deep snow, scree, brush, streams, thin ice, drifts, meltwater | **2** squares per square |
| **Blocked** | painted Blocked | crevasses, gorges, open water, chasms, rubble | can't be entered |

Rock, cliffs and walls are walls on the map: you can't move through them *and* you can't see
through them.

> **On the board.** The GM's **Terrain** filter shows the painted ground. The movement ruler
> counts Difficult squares double, so a Guide with Move 6 can cross three squares of deep snow.
> The server refuses a move into Blocked ground or through a wall. Players see the ground only
> where they can see.

### 4.1 Bad footing

- **Ice and scree.** If you **Dash** across thin ice or scree, roll **Footing 8**. Fail and you
  stop where you are, **Prone**.
- **Prone.** Standing up costs half your Move (3 squares). Attacks against a Prone creature next
  to it have Edge; from farther away, Snag.
- **Shoved off.** A creature pushed into a crevasse, gorge or open water saves **Footing 8** to
  catch the edge: it stops in the last square before the drop, **Prone**. On a failure it falls:
  **2d6** damage and it's out of the scene, hanging on the rope or under the ice, until someone
  hauls it out (an Action and **Grit 10**, or Grit 8 with a rope already on it). A creature that
  falls into open water is also **Restrained** and takes **1d6** cold at the start of each of its
  turns until it's out.

### 4.2 Rope

Guides carry 50 ft of rope each.

- **Roped together.** Guides who rope up move at the pace of the slowest of them and must end
  each turn within 2 squares of the next one on the rope. In return, none of them rolls Footing
  for bad ground, and a roped Guide who would fall is caught by the next one on the rope: the
  catcher saves **Grit 8**, and on a success nobody falls.
- **Fixing a line** across a gap of up to 3 squares (a crevasse, a stream) takes 10 minutes out of
  combat and a **Ropework** or **Lore 8** check. Then anyone can cross it at a cost of 2 squares per
  square.

> **On the board.** A fixed line is a GM call: drag the token across. The ruler still shows the
> gap as Blocked.

### 4.3 Temporary ground

The mountain changes. When the snow slides or the ice breaks, the GM paints the change on the
board as a **temporary terrain effect** with a name, and removes it when it clears.

| Effect | Ground | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| **Avalanche** | Blocked where the snow piles, Difficult at its edges | the Storm Clock, loud noise, the adventure |
| **Rockfall** | Blocked | the Cairn-Taker, the Switchbacks |
| **Cracking ice** | Blocked (holes) | weight on thin ice; spreads each round |
| **Snowdrift** | Difficult, or Blocked across a door | wind; the Hospice |
| **Ice Wall** | Blocked, and you can see through it | the Stonespeaker's art |
| **Churned snow** | Difficult | the Stonespeaker's art |

> **On the board.** **Paint terrain → New effect**, name it, paint the squares. Effects sit on top
> of the map's own ground: Blocked wins, and Difficult never stacks past double. Remove the effect
> when it ends.

## 5. Sight

You can only fight what you can see, and up here the mountain decides how far that is.

### 5.1 Weather sets the distance

At the start of each **watch** (a quarter of a day), the GM rolls **2d6** and adds one for each
filled segment of the **Storm Clock** (§7):

| Roll | Weather | How far you can see |
|---|---|---|
| 2–6 | **Clear** | as far as the map goes |
| 7–8 | **Haze** | 60 ft (12 squares) |
| 9–10 | **Snowfall** | 30 ft (6 squares) |
| 11–12 | **Squall** | 15 ft (3 squares) |
| 13+ | **Whiteout** | 10 ft (2 squares) |

Underground or at night, it's how far your light reaches: a storm lantern **30 ft**, a torch
**20 ft**, nothing at all **5 ft**.

In **Snowfall** and worse, ranged attacks at more than 6 squares have a Snag. In a **Whiteout**,
every Sense check has a Snag.

> **On the board.** Turn **line of sight** on for every scene (**Manage → Edit map → Sight**) and
> set the **sight range** to the weather in feet. Clear means no range. When the weather turns,
> change the range: every player's view changes at once.

### 5.2 What's in the way

- **Rock, cliffs and walls** block sight completely.
- **Doors** block sight while closed.
- **Ice** — ice walls, frozen falls, pressure ridges on a lake — is clear. You can see through
  it, but you can't pass it.
- **Obscuring ground** (pine stands, steam from the hot springs underground):
  nobody outside it can see into it, and from inside you see out. A Guide hidden in the pines is
  invisible to everything outside them.
- **Partial cover** (a boulder or a half wall between you and the target, but not all the way):
  Snag on the attack.

> **On the board.** Rock and walls are the map's walls. Ice is imported as **locked windows**:
> windows never block sight, and players can't open a locked one. Obscuring ground is **fog**:
> fog blocks sight from outside, and a token standing in a fog region sees out of it. Hidden
> foes and players' sight are handled by the server; nobody's screen shows what their tokens
> can't see.

### 5.3 Spotting and hiding

- Foes the Guides haven't seen are **hidden tokens**. A foe that moves into a Guide's sight is
  spotted: the GM reveals it.
- Some things lie still in the snow (white-coat wolves, a Hunter in her white hide). Coming into
  sight isn't enough: a Guide who looks rolls **Sense 8** (Snag in Snowfall or worse). On a
  success the GM reveals it.
- **Hiding** takes an Action, somewhere no foe can see you, in snow, scrub or shadow, and a
  **Footing 8** roll (Stealth applies). You stay hidden until you attack, shout or move into the
  open.

> **On the board.** A hidden creature's token is **Hidden** (players can't see it) and carries
> the **Invisible** condition. Reveal it by unticking Hidden.

### 5.4 Cairns

A standing cairn is a mark on the safe way, and a raised one pushes the white back.

- **Raising a cairn** (restacking a fallen one) takes an **Action** and **Lore 8** (Cairn-lore
  applies; Snag in a Whiteout).
- While the Guides are within 6 squares of a raised cairn, the weather counts one step better
  for them (Whiteout becomes Squall, Squall becomes Snowfall, and so on).
- Some things up here hate a raised cairn (see the bestiary).

> **On the board.** Cairns are tokens with two pictures: **Cairn (fallen)** and **Cairn
> (raised)**. Raise one by changing its image. When the Guides are near a raised cairn, set the
> sight range one step better.

## 6. Combat

### 6.1 Order

When a fight starts, everyone rolls **2d6 + Footing** for initiative. Foes use their listed
initiative. Highest goes first.

> **On the board.** **Start encounter** and type each score. For a pack of wolves, select their
> group and give them one score.

### 6.2 Your turn

On your turn you have:

- a **Move** of 6 squares, paid at the ground's cost;
- an **Action**: attack, Dash (move again), use an art, raise a cairn, haul someone out, help,
  hide, search;
- a **Quick action**: draw or stow, open or close a door, drink, Mark, call out;
- and once per round, a **Reaction** on someone else's turn, if something gives you one.

### 6.3 Attacking

Roll **2d6 + trait** against the target's **Guard**:

- **melee** with Grit, or Footing for a light weapon (ice axe, knife, spear);
- **ranged** with Sense;
- **arts** with Lore.

From level 3, add +1 to every attack.

On a hit, roll the weapon's damage and add the same trait. A **12** on the dice hits whatever the
Guard and adds one more damage die. A **2** misses.

| Weapon | Damage | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Knife | 1d4 | light, can be thrown 4 squares |
| Ice axe | 1d6 | light; Edge on climbing |
| Hatchet | 1d6 | can be thrown 4 squares |
| Spear | 1d6 | light, reach 2 squares |
| Mattock | 1d8 | two hands |
| Sling | 1d4 | range 10 squares |
| Bow | 1d8 | two hands, range 16 squares |
| Crossbow | 1d8 | range 12 squares, an Action to reload |

> **On the board.** Each Guide's token carries their weapons and arts as **named attacks**, with
> the dice already in them (`2d6+3` to hit, `1d6+3` damage). Roll from the token; the GM rules
> the hit against the target's AC (its Guard) and applies the damage.

### 6.4 Down in the snow

At **0 HP** you drop, **Unconscious**. At the start of each of your turns, roll **2d6**:

- **2–6:** mark a **Frost**. At three Frost, the mountain takes you.
- **7–11:** you hold on.
- **12:** you come round with 1 HP.

An ally next to you can **Haul Out** (an Action and **Lore 8**, Medicine applies): you stop rolling
and wake with 1 HP. Any healing does the same. Frost marks clear after a long rest.

## 7. Warmth and the Storm Clock

**Warmth** is the party's shared reserve of heat, food and dry socks: **10**.

- Each **watch** outdoors costs **1 Warmth**, or **2** in a Squall or Whiteout. A watch indoors
  beside a fire costs nothing.
- A **short rest** (an hour out of the wind) costs **1 Warmth**.
- A fire at the hospice or the guildhouse refills it.
- At **0 Warmth**, everyone has a Snag on every check, and at the end of each watch every Guide
  takes **1d6** cold.

The **Storm Clock** is the GM's: six segments that fill as the weather closes in.

- One segment at the end of every watch outdoors.
- One for noise: a shout across a slope, a crossbow volley, a rockfall.
- One when a check fails and the GM says the mountain noticed.

Each filled segment adds 1 to the weather roll. **At six**, the pass closes: Whiteout for the rest
of the day, and an avalanche wherever the GM likes.

> **On the board.** Two tokens with an HP bar: **Warmth** (10/10), where everyone can see it, and
> the **Storm Clock** (0/6), **hidden**. Change their HP as they go up and down.

## 8. Rest and advancement

- **Short rest:** an hour, 1 Warmth. Recover half your HP (round up) and your arts' Stones.
- **Long rest:** a night at the hospice or the guildhouse. Recover everything; clear Frost.

| Level | When | You gain |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | a rescue made | HP (your calling's step), one new art |
| 3 | a second rescue | +1 to a trait (max +4), +1 to all attacks |
| 4 | the pass made safe | HP (your calling's step), one new art |

## 9. Callings

### 9.1 Pathfinder

*You read the snow. You go first, and you find the way the others can follow.*

- **+1 Footing.** HP **10 + 2 × Grit**, +4 a level.
- **Gear:** ice axe, sling, spyglass, rope, crampons.
- **Feature — Read the Weather.** Once per watch, before the GM rolls the weather, roll
  **Sense 8**. On a success the GM rolls twice and you choose.
- **Arts:**
  - **Mark** (*Quick*). A foe you can see is marked until the start of your next turn. Everyone
    has Edge against it.
  - **Spyglass** (*Action*). Roll **Sense 8**. On a success the GM reveals every hidden creature in
    your line of sight, even past the sight range.
  - **Find the Line** (*Action*). Name a path of up to 6 squares through Difficult ground. Until
    your next turn, the Guides who follow it pay Normal cost.
- **Level 2 (choose one):** *Sure-foot* — you never roll for bad footing. *Long Sight* — the
  weather counts one step better for you alone.
- **Level 4 (choose one):** *Lead the Rope* — roped Guides move at your pace. *Call the Gap* — once
  per scene, one Guide who would fall doesn't.

> **On the board.** Named attacks: *Ice axe* (to-hit `2d6 + Footing`, damage `1d6 + Footing`),
> *Sling* (`2d6 + Sense`, `1d4 + Sense`).

### 9.2 Hauler

*You carry the camp, hold the rope and stand where the wind is worst.*

- **+1 Grit.** HP **14 + 2 × Grit**, +6 a level.
- **Gear:** mattock, heavy coat (+1 Guard), the camp kettle, 100 ft of rope, a shield of
  lashed boards (+1 Guard).
- **Feature — Packhorse.** The party's Warmth is **12** while you're with it.
- **Arts:**
  - **Shove** (*Action*). A melee attack with no damage. On a hit, push the target 1 square, or 2
    if it's smaller than you. Into a crevasse, it saves to catch the edge (§4.1).
  - **Anchor** (*Action*). Throw a loop of rope: a melee attack with no damage, reach 2. On a hit
    the target is **Restrained** until it breaks free (**Grit 10**, its Action).
  - **Belay** (*Reaction*). An ally within 3 squares who would fall doesn't.
- **Level 2 (choose one):** *Brace* — while you don't move, foes can't push you or the ally beside
  you. *Second Wind* — once per rest, regain 1d6 + Grit HP as a Quick action.
- **Level 4 (choose one):** *Dig In* — Difficult ground costs you nothing extra. *Haul* — Haul Out
  is a Quick action for you.

> **On the board.** Named attacks: *Mattock* (`2d6 + Grit`, `1d8 + Grit`), *Shove* and *Anchor*
> (to-hit only).

### 9.3 Hunter

*You feed the camp and keep the wolves honest. They never see you first.*

- **+1 Sense.** HP **10 + 2 × Grit**, +5 a level.
- **Gear:** bow, spear, white hide cloak, snares, knife.
- **Feature — Ambush.** When you attack a creature that couldn't see you, add **1d6** damage.
- **Arts:**
  - **White Hide** (*Action*). In snow or scrub, where no foe can see you: you're hidden, no roll.
  - **Twin Shot** (*Action*). Two arrows at two targets, each with a Snag.
  - **Snare** (*Action*). A ranged attack with no damage, range 6. On a hit the target is
    **Restrained** until it frees itself (**Footing 10**, its Action).
- **Level 2 (choose one):** *Tracker* — Edge on every Sense check to follow tracks; *Patient* — while
  hidden you can attack once without being revealed.
- **Level 4 (choose one):** *Heartshot* — Ambush adds 2d6; *Hunter's Mark* — your Mark lasts the whole
  scene (you learn Mark).

> **On the board.** Named attacks: *Bow* (`2d6 + Sense`, `1d8 + Sense`), *Twin Shot* (`2d6 +
> Sense − 1`, `1d8 + Sense`, **up to 2 targets**), *Spear*, *Snare* (to-hit only), *Ambush* (damage
> `1d6` only).

### 9.4 Stonespeaker

*The old stones listen to you. You ask the ice to move, and sometimes it does.*

- **+1 Lore.** HP **8 + 2 × Grit**, +4 a level.
- **Gear:** staff, a pouch of cairn-stones, a hearth-stone that stays warm.
- **Stones.** You have **3 Stones**. An art with a cost spends them. They come back after a rest.
- **Arts** (the Art DC is **7 + Lore**):
  - **Stone Fist** (*Action*, free). A ranged attack, range 8: `2d6 + Lore`, `1d6 + Lore`.
  - **Rime Lash** (*Action*, 1 Stone). A 15 ft cone of killing cold. Everyone in it saves **Grit**
    against your Art DC: **2d6** damage, half on a save.
  - **Ice Wall** (*Action*, 1 Stone). Raise a wall of clear ice up to 4 squares long, in a
    straight line, within 6 squares. Nothing passes it; everyone sees through it. It lasts the
    scene, or until it takes 10 damage.
  - **Churn the Snow** (*Action*, 1 Stone). A 3 × 3 area within 8 squares becomes deep snow
    (Difficult) for the scene.
  - **Cairnsong** (*Action*, 1 Stone). One Guide within 6 squares regains **1d6 + Lore** HP. Beside a
    raised cairn, every Guide within 2 squares of it does.
- **Level 2 (choose one):** *Deep Stones* — 4 Stones. *Hold the Ice* — keep an Ice Wall or Churn
  going into the next scene (you're **Concentrating**).
- **Level 4 (choose one):** *Avalanche Call* — once per day, 2 Stones: a 2 × 6 area becomes an
  avalanche (§4.3); everyone in it saves **Footing** against your Art DC or takes **2d6** and is
  **Restrained**. *Stone Ward* — when an ally within 6 squares is hit, spend 1 Stone to give them +2
  Guard against it.

> **On the board.** Named attacks: *Stone Fist*, *Rime Lash* (damage `2d6` only), *Cairnsong*
> (damage `1d6 + Lore` only: apply it as healing). For **Ice Wall** and **Churn the Snow**, the GM
> paints a temporary effect (§4.3) with that name.

## 10. Gear

Everyone carries: rope (50 ft), a storm lantern, a bedroll, crampons, three days' food, flint and
steel, a tin cup. Your calling adds the rest.

| Item | Use |
|---|---|
| Storm lantern | light to 30 ft underground or at night |
| Crampons | Edge on Footing checks on ice |
| Spyglass | the Pathfinder's art; anyone else, Edge on Sense at long range |
| Snow-goggles | ignore the Snag from Snowfall on Sense checks |
| Tonic of hot gin | regain 1d6 HP, Quick action, 2 in the camp kettle |

## 11. Conditions

The board's conditions, with what they mean up here. Charmed and Paralyzed don't come up.

| Condition | Up here | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Blinded** | snow-blind | You can't see; attacks you make have Snag 2, attacks against you Edge |
| **Frightened** | shaken | Snag on attacks and checks while the cause is in sight |
| **Grappled** | held | Your Move is 0 |
| **Restrained** | snowbound, roped, snared | Move 0; Snag on attacks; attacks against you have Edge |
| **Prone** | lost footing | §4.1 |
| **Invisible** | white on white | Hidden: see §5.3 |
| **Poisoned** | frost-sick | Snag on every check |
| **Stunned** | winded | No Action or Reaction, half Move, until the end of your next turn |
| **Unconscious** | down | §6.4 |
| **Concentrating** | holding an art | When you take damage, save **Grit 8** or the art ends |

## 12. For the GM

### 12.1 Running the mountain

Each scene, decide three things before the Guides arrive, and set them on the board:

1. **The ground.** The map's own ground is painted already if you set the game up with the
   setup script; check it with the Terrain filter.
2. **The weather.** Roll it, and set the sight range.
3. **What waits, and where.** Hidden tokens for whatever hasn't been seen yet.

Then let the board do the work. Deep snow slows everyone, the ruler shows it, and the Guides will
start choosing their ground before you ask them to.

### 12.2 Threat

| Foe | Threat |
|---|---|
| White-coat, Scree goat, Rimehand | 1 |
| Ice-crawler swarm | 2 |
| Kettle Varga | 4 |
| The Cairn-Taker | 10 |

For four level-1 Guides: **4** is a fair fight, **6** a hard one, **9** a deadly one. Add 2 at
level 2. Bad ground and bad weather count for more than numbers: four wolves in a Whiteout are a
deadly fight.

### 12.3 Making it fair

- **Say what the ground is** before anyone moves onto it. Guides should die of their choices, not
  of a square they didn't know was thin ice.
- **Telegraph the mountain.** Before an avalanche or a rockfall, there's a sound. Give the Guides
  a round to react.
- **Let them use it.** A Hauler who shoves a Rimehand into a crevasse has won the fight; let them.
